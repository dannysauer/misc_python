Closed-loop simulation of the setpoint-pair control, captured 2026-11-15.

Command, from the project directory:

    uv run python tests/closed_loop.py 10

What it runs: a simulated house with heavy walls and an auto-mode thermostat
on a schedule of two comfort settings, Home at 20 °C from 06:00 to 21:00 and
Sleep at 17.5 °C otherwise, with a differential of 2 °C. The thermostat starts
its equipment 0.25 °C past a setpoint and stops it 0.25 °C inside. Thirty days
on a fixed heat-preference pair train the model; the ten days after are
measured.

"Fixed" rows have no tool: at each change of comfort setting the thermostat
goes to that kind of pair for the new target and stays there. In the other
rows the thermostat does the same with the heat-preference pair, as an ecobee
whose holds end at the next activity would, and the tool runs in that same
five-minute step and every ten minutes after, through the same steer() the
command line calls. The forecast given to the tool is the weather that then
happens, so forecast error plays no part. "min ahead" is
decision.overshoot_minutes, and "no hold" is decision.min_hold_minutes = 0.

"Off target" is the distance between the indoor temperature and the target of
the comfort setting in force, over every five-minute step. "A new target
reached" is the longest the house took, after a change of comfort setting, to
come within 0.25 °C of the new target; 540 and 900 minutes are the lengths of
the two comfort settings, and mean the target was not reached before the next
change. "Decisions" counts the tool's runs by the rule that decided, with the
settling rule split into drift and drive. "Equipment ran against a drift"
counts the steps in which the equipment a drift was meant to keep off ran
(HVP-015).

What it does not show: the three climates here seldom let the house drift to
a lower night-time target in time, so the drift pair is exercised mostly on
the way up in the morning. tests/test_closed_loop.py covers the evening drift
in a colder climate.
